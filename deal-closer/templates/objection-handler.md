# Objection Handler

> Every objection is a request for more information. Acknowledge → Reframe → Advance.

## The universal framework
1. **Acknowledge** — "That's a fair concern." (never argue)
2. **Clarify** — "Help me understand what's behind that."
3. **Reframe** — Reframe as cost of inaction or missing opportunity
4. **Advance** — "What would have to be true for us to move forward?"

## Common objections and responses

### "Too expensive"
**Acknowledge:** "I hear you — budget is always a consideration."
**Clarify:** "When you say expensive, are you comparing to a specific alternative, or is it about the total budget impact?"
**Reframe:** "What's the cost of NOT solving [PAIN]? If [PAIN] costs you [QUANTIFIED: $X/month, Y hours/week, Z% churn], our solution pays for itself in [TIMEFRAME]."
**Advance:** "If I can show the ROI math, would that change the conversation?"

### "We're happy with our current vendor"
**Acknowledge:** "That's great — it means you've already invested in solving this."
**Clarify:** "What would have to be true for you to consider switching? Is there a gap you've accepted?"
**Reframe:** "Most of our customers were 'happy' with their previous solution until they saw [SPECIFIC_IMPROVEMENT]. The question isn't whether your current tool works — it's whether it's the best use of your budget."
**Advance:** "Would a quick comparison be worth 15 minutes?"

### "Need to talk to my team / decision maker"
**Acknowledge:** "Absolutely — this should be a team decision."
**Clarify:** "Who else needs to be involved? What's their perspective on [PAIN]?"
**Reframe:** "I'd love to join that conversation. I can answer technical questions and address concerns in real-time. Would that help?"
**Advance:** "Can I draft a one-pager your team can review?"

### "Now isn't the right time"
**Acknowledge:** "Timing is everything — I respect that."
**Clarify:** "When would be better? What changes between now and then?"
**Reframe:** "I understand. Just to flag: [PAIN] tends to [GET WORSE / COST MORE] over time. The teams that solve it earlier tend to [ADVANTAGE]."
**Advance:** "Can we set a reminder for [DATE] and I'll check in?"

### "We tried something similar before"
**Acknowledge:** "That's valuable experience — you know what doesn't work."
**Clarify:** "What was different about that attempt? What would need to be true this time?"
**Reframe:** "Our approach is specifically designed to avoid [COMMON_FAILURE]. Here's how we're different: [SPECIFIC_DIFFERENTIATOR]."
**Advance:** "Would a proof-of-concept on a small scope de-risk this for you?"

### "I need to think about it"
**Acknowledge:** "Of course — this is an important decision."
**Clarify:** "What specifically are you weighing? Is it [COST / TIMING / COMPARISON / CONFIDENCE]?"
**Reframe:** "Totally fair. Just to help you think: the main things to evaluate are [CRITERIA_1, CRITERIA_2, CRITERIA_3]. We score well on all three."
**Advance:** "Can we reconnect [DATE] with any questions that come up?"

### "Send me more information"
**Acknowledge:** "Happy to — I want you to have everything you need."
**Clarify:** "What specifically would be most useful? A case study? Technical specs? ROI calculator?"
**Reframe:** "I'll send [TARGETED_ASSET], but I find a 15-minute call is usually more efficient than 47 pages of PDF."
**Advance:** "Can we block 15 minutes while I compile this?"

## Rules of engagement
- Never argue with the objection
- Always clarify before responding
- Quantify the cost of inaction whenever possible
- End every response with an advance (next step)
- If the objection is a deal-breaker, acknowledge it gracefully and move on

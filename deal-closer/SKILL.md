## SECTION 1: PIPELINE MATH (Know Your Numbers)

### Revenue Waterfall Formula
Pipeline Required = Quarterly Quota / Win Rate / Average Discount
Example: $500K quota / 25% win rate / 10% discount = $2.22M pipeline needed

### Pipeline Velocity
Velocity = (# Deals in Pipeline) x (Win Rate) x (Avg Deal Size) / (Sales Cycle Length)
Example: 50 deals x 25% x $20K / 90 days = $2,777/day = $250K/month

### Conversion Rate Benchmarks (SaaS)
Lead to MQL: 20-30%
MQL to SQL: 30-40%
SQL to Opportunity: 40-50%
Opportunity to Closed Won: 20-30%
Overall Lead to Close: 2-5%

### Cycle Time Benchmarks
SMB (<$10K ACV): 14-30 days
Mid-Market ($10K-$50K): 30-90 days
Enterprise ($50K+): 90-180 days
Strategic ($500K+): 180-365 days

### Activity Benchmarks (SDR)
Dials per day: 50-75
Conversations per day: 8-12
Meetings booked per day: 2-3
Meetings show rate: 60-70%
Meetings to qualified: 40-50%

### Activity Benchmarks (AE)
Demos per week: 8-12
Proposals per week: 4-6
Closes per month: 4-8
Quota attainment at 100%: 4x OTE

### Quota Math
AE OTE (On-Target Earnings): $150K-$250K
AE Base Salary: 50% of OTE ($75K-$125K)
AE Variable: 50% of OTE ($75K-$125K)
AE Quota: 4-5x OTE ($600K-$1.25M)
Commission Rate: 10-15% on new business
Accelerators: 1.5x at 100% quota, 2x at 120%, 3x at 150%

SDR OTE: $60K-$80K
SDR Base: 60% of OTE ($36K-$48K)
SDR Variable: 40% of OTE ($24K-$32K)
SDR Quota: 40-60 qualified meetings per quarter
Bonus: $50-$150 per qualified meeting

## SECTION 2: COLD OUTREACH (Exact Scripts)

### Cold Email Sequence (5 emails over 14 days)

Email 1 (Day 1) -- Pattern Interrupt:
Subject: [First Name], quick question about [specific pain point]
Body:
Hi [First Name],
I noticed [specific observation about their company -- funding, hiring, product launch].
Companies at this stage usually struggle with [pain point].
We helped [similar company] achieve [specific result -- "$X in Y months"].
Would 15 minutes this week make sense to explore if we can do the same?
[Your Name]

Email 2 (Day 3) -- Value Add:
Subject: Re: [Original Subject]
Body:
Hi [First Name],
Wanted to share something that might help. We published a case study on how
[similar company] solved [pain point] -- here is the link: [URL]
Quick takeaway: They reduced [metric] by [X]% in [Y] months.
Happy to walk through the details if useful.
[Your Name]

Email 3 (Day 7) -- Social Proof:
Subject: Re: [Original Subject]
Body:
Hi [First Name],
Since my last email, [another similar company] also started using our solution.
They are seeing [specific result] within [timeframe].
Not sure if this is a priority for you right now, but wanted to keep you in the loop.
[Your Name]

Email 4 (Day 10) -- Breakup:
Subject: Should I close your file?
Body:
Hi [First Name],
I have reached out a few times and have not heard back.
I assume this is not a priority right now -- totally fine.
Would it be okay if I circle back in [3-6 months]?
[Your Name]

Email 5 (Day 14) -- Final Value:
Subject: Last thought, [First Name]
Body:
Hi [First Name],
One last thought: [share a relevant insight or data point about their industry].
If this ever becomes a priority, I am here.
Wishing you and the team a great quarter.
[Your Name]

### Cold Call Script (60 seconds)

Opener (10 seconds):
"Hi [First Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company]. I know I am calling out
of the blue -- do you have 30 seconds for me to tell you why I am calling
and you can decide if it is worth continuing?"

[If yes -- continue. If no -- leave voicemail.]

Pain (20 seconds):
"The reason I am calling is that companies like [similar company] were struggling
with [specific pain point]. They were losing [X hours/dollars] per [month/quarter]
because of it."

Bridge (15 seconds):
"We built a solution that helps them [specific result -- reduced X by Y%].
I thought this might be relevant for you."

Close (15 seconds):
"Would it make sense to set up a 15-minute call to see if we can do
the same for you? How does [specific day] look?"

### LinkedIn Connection Request (300 characters max):
"Hi [First Name] -- I help [type of company] solve [specific pain point].
We recently helped [similar company] achieve [result]. Would love to connect
and share some insights that might be useful for your team."

### LinkedIn Follow-Up Message (after connection):
"Thanks for connecting, [First Name]. I wanted to share a quick insight:
[one specific, valuable observation about their business].
We have helped companies in your space achieve [specific result].
Would a 15-minute call make sense to explore this?"

## SECTION 3: DISCOVERY CALL (30-Minute Script)

### Pre-Call Research Checklist (5 minutes before call)
[ ] LinkedIn profile review (title, tenure, posts, mutual connections)
[ ] Company website review (product, pricing, news, funding)
[ ] Recent news (funding, layoffs, product launches, hiring)
[ ] Tech stack (BuiltWith, LinkedIn, job postings)
[ ] Competitor landscape (who are they using now?)
[ ] Decision-making process (who else is involved?)

### Discovery Call Script

Minutes 0-2: Rapport
"Thanks for taking the time, [First Name]. Before we dive in, I want to make
sure this call is valuable for you. What is the one thing you want to walk
away with from this conversation?"

Minutes 2-5: Situation (SPIN-S)
"Walk me through your current process for [relevant activity]."
"How long have you been doing it this way?"
"Who else is involved in this process?"
"What tools are you currently using?"

Minutes 5-10: Problem (SPIN-P)
"What is the biggest challenge you face with [current process]?"
"How often does this problem occur?"
"What have you tried so far to solve it?"
"What happens when this problem is not resolved?"

Minutes 10-15: Implication (SPIN-I) -- MOST POWERFUL
"What is the impact on your team when [problem] happens?"
"How much time/money is this costing you per [month/quarter]?"
"If this continues for another [6-12 months], what does that look like?"
"What does this mean for your personal goals/OKRs?"

Minutes 15-20: Need-Payoff (SPIN-N)
"If we could solve [problem], what would that mean for your team?"
"How would it change your day-to-day?"
"What would you do with the [time/money] you save?"
"On a scale of 1-10, how important is solving this?"

Minutes 20-25: Qualification (MEDDPICC quick check)
"Who else would need to be involved in evaluating a solution like this?"
"What does your decision process look like?"
"What is your timeline for making a change?"
"What budget have you allocated for this?"

Minutes 25-30: Next Steps
"I think there is a real fit here. Here is what I suggest:
1. I will put together a customized demo based on what we discussed
2. You bring [specific stakeholder] to see how this impacts them
3. We walk through ROI together
Does [specific day/time] work for the demo?"

## SECTION 4: DEMO SCRIPT (30-Minute Demo)

### Demo Structure
Minutes 0-5: Recap and agenda
"Last time, you mentioned [pain point] was costing you [impact].
Today I will show you exactly how we solve this.
Here is the agenda -- does this look right?"

Minutes 5-15: Solution (tailored to THEIR pain)
NOT: "Let me show you all our features"
INSTEAD: "You mentioned [pain]. Here is exactly how we solve it."
[Show specific workflow that addresses their pain]
"See how this would work for your [specific use case]?"

Minutes 15-20: ROI Calculator
"Let me show you the numbers.
Current cost of [problem]: $[X]/month
Time lost: [Y] hours x $[Z]/hour = $[A]
With our solution: $[B]/month
Net savings: $[C]/month = $[D]/year
ROI: [E]x return in year one"

Minutes 20-25: Social Proof
"Here is what [similar company] achieved after implementing this:
[Specific metric] improved by [X]% within [Y] months.
[Name] at [Company] said: [testimonial]"
[Share 1-2 relevant case studies]

Minutes 25-30: Close
"Based on what you have seen, how does this align with what you need?"
"On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you this solves your problem?"
"What would need to be true for us to move forward this week?"

## SECTION 5: OBJECTION HANDLING (Word-for-Word)

### Price Objections
"Too expensive"
"It sounds like price is a concern. Let me ask -- compared to the cost of
NOT solving [pain], which is higher? You mentioned [pain] costs you $[X]/month.
Our solution is $[Y]/month. That is a [Z]x return. Does that math make sense?"

"We do not have budget"
"I hear you. Budget is often tight. Let me ask -- if I could show you a way to
fund this from the savings it creates, would that change the conversation?
Many of our clients use the [cost savings/revenue gains] to justify the investment."

### Timing Objections
"Not the right time"
"I understand timing is everything. Let me ask -- what happens if you wait
[6 months]? You mentioned [pain] costs you $[X]/month. That is $[6X] over
6 months. Is waiting worth $[6X]?"

"I need to think about it"
"Of course. What specifically do you want to think about? Is it the ROI?
The implementation? The team buy-in? Let me address your concerns now
so you have everything you need."

### Authority Objections
"I need to talk to my boss"
"Totally understand. What does your boss care about most? Is it ROI?
Risk reduction? Team productivity? Let me give you exactly what you need
to make the case. Would it help if I joined that conversation?"

### Competition Objections
"We already have a solution"
"That is great -- it means you understand the value. What is missing from
your current solution? [Listen]. Here is specifically how we address that.
[Show differentiator]."

"We are talking to [Competitor]"
"Good -- it means you are doing your homework. Here is what our clients
tell us distinguishes us: [specific differentiator with proof].
Can I show you a side-by-side comparison?"

### Trust Objections
"I have never heard of you"
"Fair point. Let me share something that might help:
[Specific case study with named client and metrics].
We have [X] clients in your industry who faced the same challenge."

### No Objection
"Just not interested"
"I respect that. Most of our best clients said the same thing initially.
Can I ask -- is it the timing, the priority, or the solution itself?
I want to make sure I am not wasting your time."

## SECTION 6: QUALIFICATION (MEDDPICC Deep Dive)

### Exact Scoring Rubric (1-5 per criterion)

M -- Metrics (1-5):
1 = No metrics identified
2 = Vague outcomes mentioned
3 = Some metrics identified, not quantified
4 = Clear metrics, partially quantified
5 = Specific $ impact, agreed upon by prospect

E -- Economic Buyer (1-5):
1 = Do not know who they are
2 = Name only, no access
3 = Identified, some access
4 = Have met, responsive
5 = Engaged, advocates internally

D -- Decision Criteria (1-5):
1 = Unknown
2 = Partially known
3 = Mostly known, not documented
4 = Documented, we meet most criteria
5 = We helped define the criteria

D -- Decision Process (1-5):
1 = Unknown
2 = Rough outline
3 = Steps identified, some dates
4 = Detailed steps with dates
5 = Formal process, we are on the timeline

P -- Paper Process (1-5):
1 = Unknown
2 = Vague awareness
3 = Know the process, not the details
4 = Know the process and key contacts
5 = Have met legal/procurement, terms agreed

I -- Implicate Pain (1-5):
1 = No pain identified
2 = Surface-level pain
3 = Pain quantified partially
4 = Pain quantified, business impact clear
5 = Pain is urgent, prospect is motivated

C -- Champion (1-5):
1 = No champion
2 = Friendly contact
3 = Supports us, shares information
4 = Actively selling internally
5 = Has power + influence + willingness to sell

C -- Competition (1-5):
1 = Do not know who else is bidding
2 = Know some competitors
3 = Know all competitors, our advantages clear
4 = Have a battle plan for each competitor
5 = Prospect has told us we are the front-runner

### Gate Thresholds
Total >= 32: STRONG -- pursue aggressively, accelerate timeline
Total 24-31: DEVELOPING -- fill gaps before advancing
Total 16-23: WEAK -- significant gaps, re-evaluate
Total < 16: STOP -- likely waste of time

## SECTION 7: COMPETITIVE BATTLE CARDS

### How to Beat Salesforce
Their weakness: Complexity, slow implementation, expensive customization
Your angle: "Salesforce is powerful but complex. We deliver [X] in [Y weeks]
instead of [Z months]. Here is the TCO comparison over 3 years:"
[Show total cost: license + implementation + customization + admin + training]

### How to Beat HubSpot
Their weakness: Limited customization, reporting gaps, enterprise features
Your angle: "HubSpot is great for starting out. But when you need [advanced
feature], you hit a ceiling. We built specifically for [use case].
Here is the feature comparison:"

### How to Beat "Do Nothing"
Their weakness: Status quo bias, inertia
Your angle: "I understand doing nothing feels safe. But let me show you the
cost of inaction: $[X]/month in lost [revenue/time/efficiency].
Over 12 months, that is $[12X]. Our solution pays for itself in [Y] months."

### How to Beat a Lower-Cost Competitor
Their weakness: Limited features, poor support, scalability issues
Your angle: "Price is important. But let me show you the total cost:
Theirs: $[A]/month + $[B] in hidden costs (implementation, support, lost productivity)
Ours: $[C]/month + $[D] in savings (faster onboarding, better support)
Net difference: $[E]/month in YOUR favor with us."

## SECTION 8: NEGOTIATION (Voss + Belfort Tactics)

### Mirroring (Voss)
When they say: "We need to reduce the price by 30%"
You say: "Reduce the price by 30%?"
[Pause. Let them elaborate. They will explain their real concern.]

### Labeling (Voss)
"It sounds like you are worried about [implementation risk]"
"It seems like the budget is the main constraint"
"It feels like there is pressure from [stakeholder]"

### Calibration Questions (Voss)
"How am I supposed to do that?"
"What would it take to make this work for both sides?"
"How should we structure this so your boss approves?"

### Tactical Empathy (Voss)
Acknowledge their perspective before presenting yours:
"I can see why you would feel that way. From your position, [restate their view].
Let me share how I have seen other [role] handle this same situation."

### Belfort Certainty Loop
Check certainty on all 3 dimensions:
Product certainty: "On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you this solves [pain]?"
Company certainty: "How comfortable are you with our track record?"
Personal certainty: "Do you feel I understand your situation?"

If any is below 80%, loop back:
"Let me address that directly..." [address specific concern]
"What would it take to get you to [X]?"

## SECTION 9: CLOSING SEQUENCES

### The Assumptive Close
"Great. Let me send over the agreement. What is the best email for the contract?"

### The Summary Close
"So to recap: We solve [pain], you get [result], the investment is $[X],
and the ROI is [Y]x. Shall we move forward?"

### The Now-or-Never Close
"This pricing is valid through [specific date]. After that, it goes up to $[Y].
Given the timeline you mentioned, it makes sense to lock this in now."

### The Question Close
"What would need to be true for us to start onboarding next week?"

### The Puppy Dog Close
"Let us set up a 30-day pilot. You use it, see the results, and then decide.
No risk, no commitment."

## SECTION 10: WIN/LOSS ANALYSIS

### Win Interview Questions
1. What was the primary reason you chose us?
2. What almost made you choose someone else?
3. What was the deciding factor?
4. How did our sales process compare to others?
5. What would you tell a colleague considering us?

### Loss Interview Questions
1. What was the primary reason you chose [competitor]?
2. What could we have done differently?
3. Was there a specific feature or capability that was missing?
4. Did our pricing influence your decision?
5. Would you consider us in the future? Why/why not?

### Loss Categories (track these)
- Lost to competitor (who? why?)
- Lost to do-nothing (why?)
- Lost to budget (real or excuse?)
- Lost to timing (when will they revisit?)
- Lost to internal solution (what did they build?)

## SECTION 11: THE COMPLETE DEAL LOOP

1. Research (30 min): Company, person, pain hypothesis, value hypothesis
2. Cold Outreach (Day 1-14): Email sequence + LinkedIn + cold calls
3. Discovery (30 min): SPIN + MEDDPICC qualification
4. Demo (30 min): Tailored to their pain, ROI calculator
5. Proposal: Custom ROI, terms, timeline
6. Negotiation: Voss + Belfort tactics
7. Close: Assume the sale, get signature
8. Handoff: Introduce to CS, set expectations
9. Win/Loss: Interview, document, improve
10. Skillify: What worked? What did not? Update playbook.

## ANTI-PATTERNS
- Talking more than listening (rep who talks least wins)
- Presenting before discovering pain (SPIN violation)
- No Champion identified (cannot win without one inside)
- Not quantifying value (ROI is not optional)
- Using BANT alone (too simple for complex sales)
- Not checkpointing before high-stakes calls
- Giving discounts without getting something in return
- Not knowing your competitive battle cards
- Not tracking pipeline math (velocity, conversion rates)
- Not having a written follow-up after every call

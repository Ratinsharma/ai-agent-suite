# deal-closer -- The Complete Sales Agent

Mandate: Close more deals by choosing the RIGHT framework for the RIGHT situation.
This skill contains every major sales framework. Use the Situation Selector to pick
which one to apply.

| Situation | Framework | Why |
|-----------|-----------|-----|
| Complex B2B, long cycle | SPIN + MEDDPICC + Challenger | Discovery + qualification + insight |
| Transactional, short cycle | Sandler + Cialdini | Quick qualify, influence |

## PART 1: DISCOVERY FRAMEWORKS

### 1. SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham, 1988)
Based on 35,000 sales calls across 23 countries.

The 4 question types (in order):
S -- Situation: Establish context. "Walk me through your current process"
P -- Problem: Identify pain. "What challenges are you facing?"
I -- Implication: Amplify pain. "What happens if you do not fix this?" (MOST POWERFUL)
N -- Need-Payoff: Prospect sells themselves. "How would it help if we could solve X?"

Key insight: Implication questions are the most powerful. They make the prospect
FEEL the pain. Need-Payoff gets them selling themselves.

When to use: Complex, high-value sales. NOT for simple transactional.

Common mistakes:
- Too many Situation questions (boring, low value)
- Skipping Implication (weak discovery, no urgency)
- Jumping to solution before establishing pain

### 2. GAP Selling (Keenan)
Core principle: Find the GAP between where they are and where they want to be.

Framework:
1. Current state: Where are they now? (pain, problems, situation)
2. Desired future state: Where do they want to be? (goals, dreams, outcomes)
3. GAP: How big is the gap between current and future?
4. Cost of inaction: What happens if they do not close the gap?
5. Plan: Your solution bridges the gap

Key insight: The BIGGER the GAP, the faster the sale. If the GAP is small,
the deal dies. Your job is to make the GAP feel huge.

When to use: Any sale where the buyer does not know how big their problem is.

### 3. Consultative Selling (Mack Hanan)
Core principle: Be a consultant, not a salesperson. Diagnose before you prescribe.

Framework:
1. Research the business (industry, competitors, financials)
2. Ask diagnostic questions (like a doctor)
3. Identify the root cause (not the symptoms)
4. Prescribe the solution (your product as medicine)
5. Quantify the ROI (cost of disease vs cost of cure)

Key insight: "The customer does not want a drill. They want a hole. They do not
want a hole. They want to hang a picture. They do not want a picture. They want
to feel proud of their home." Go deeper than surface needs.

### 4. Solution Selling (FranklinCovey)
Core principle: Sell the solution to a business problem, not a product.

Framework:
1. Identify the business problem
2. Quantify the cost of the problem
3. Build consensus around the problem (multiple stakeholders agree)
4. Present the solution (your product as the answer)
5. Prove the ROI (quantified value)

Key insight: If you cannot quantify the business problem in dollars, you do
not have a sale. You have a conversation.

## PART 2: QUALIFICATION FRAMEWORKS

### 5. MEDDPICC (Dick Dunkel, Jack Napoli)
The 8 Qualification Criteria:
M -- Metrics: What measurable outcomes? ROI? Cost savings?
E -- Economic Buyer: Who signs the check? Do I have access?
D -- Decision Criteria: How will they evaluate? What matters most?
D -- Decision Process: Steps, people involved, timeline?
P -- Paper Process: Legal, procurement, compliance?
I -- Implicate Pain: Cost of inaction? What breaks if they do nothing?
C -- Champion: Who inside is selling for me? Power + willingness?
C -- Competition: Who else are they evaluating? My advantages?

Scoring: Rate each 1-5. Total / 40 = qualification strength.
32-40: Strong, pursue aggressively.
24-31: Develop further, fill gaps.
Below 24: Likely waste of time.

### 6. BANT (IBM, classic)
B -- Budget: Do they have budget allocated?
A -- Authority: Are you talking to the decision maker?
N -- Need: Do they have a real need?
T -- Timing: When do they need to act?

Limitation: BANT is too simple for complex sales. Use MEDDPICC instead.

### 7. GPCTBA/C&I (HubSpot modern)
G -- Goals: What are they trying to achieve?
P -- Plans: What are they doing about it now?
C -- Challenges: What is stopping them?
T -- Timeline: When do they need results?
B -- Budget: What budget is available?
A -- Authority: Who makes the final decision?
C -- Consequences: What happens if they do nothing?
I -- Implications: What is the impact on the business?

When to use: Inbound leads, SaaS sales, mid-market.

### 8. Miller Heiman Strategic Selling
3 Lenses: Buyer Lens, Seller Lens, Product Lens.

4 Buyer Types:
- Economic Buyer: Cares about ROI. Quantify value.
- User Buyer: Cares about workflow. Demo, free trial.
- Technical Buyer: Cares about specs. Documentation, certs.
- Coach: Internal champion. Make them look good.

Blue Sheet Process: List stakeholders, classify by type, assess relationship
strength (1-5), identify Decision Process, map strengths vs competition.

### 9. Complex Sale (Tom Snyder)
Core principle: In complex sales, you are not selling to a person.
You are selling to a BUYING CENTER (a group of people with different agendas).

Framework:
1. Map the buying center (all stakeholders)
2. Identify the Economic Buyer (who signs)
3. Identify the Champion (who sells for you)
4. Identify the Blocker (who can kill the deal)
5. Develop strategy for each person
6. Build consensus across the group

Key insight: If you cannot name the Champion and the Blocker, you do not
understand the deal.

## PART 3: PRESENTATION AND INFLUENCE FRAMEWORKS

### 10. The Challenger Sale (Dixon and Adamson, 2011)
Based on 6,000+ sales reps.

The 5 Rep Profiles:
- Challenger (40% of top): Teaches, tailors, takes control
- Hard Worker (20%): Persistent, goes extra mile
- Lone Wolf (18%): Self-reliant, trusts instincts
- Reactive Problem Solver (14%): Delivers on promises
- Relationship Builder (7%): WORST in complex B2B

The Challenger Model:
1. Teach -- Reframe customer thinking. Share unique insights.
2. Tailor -- Customize message per stakeholder.
3. Take Control -- Guide the process. Push back respectfully.

Key insight: Relationship Builders are the WORST in complex B2B.
Challengers win because they bring INSIGHT, not just rapport.

### 11. Pitch Anything (Oren Klaff)
The STRONG Method:
S -- Set the Frame: Control the context before presenting.
T -- Tell the Story: Create intrigue, not data dump.
R -- Reveal the Intrigue: Show the gap between current and possible.
O -- Offer the Prize: Position yourself as the prize, not the supplicant.
N -- Nail the Hook: Create urgency with a time-limited offer.
G -- Get the Deal: Close with confidence.

Frame Control:
- Power Frame: "I have something you need" (not "please buy")
- Time Frame: "This opportunity is time-limited"
- Analyst Frame: "Here are the numbers" (back up with data)
- Prize Frame: "I am the prize, not you" (scarcity)

Key insight: If you are supplicating (begging for the deal), you have already lost.
You must be the prize. They must want YOU.

When to use: High-ticket, premium, complex deals. NOT for commodity sales.

### 12. Inbound Sales (HubSpot)
Core principle: Attract, engage, delight. Do not interrupt. Be found.

Framework:
1. Identify: Attract strangers (content, SEO, social)
2. Connect: Convert visitors to leads (forms, CTAs, offers)
3. Explore: Qualify inbound leads (discovery call)
4. Advise: Present solution tailored to their needs
5. Close: Convert to customer
6. Delight: Exceed expectations, generate referrals

When to use: SaaS, content-driven businesses, inbound leads.

## PART 4: NEGOTIATION FRAMEWORKS

### 13. Never Split the Difference (Chris Voss, 2016)
Based on FBI hostage negotiation.

Mirroring: Repeat last 3 words. Gets them to elaborate.
Labeling: Name their emotion. "It sounds like you are frustrated with..."
Calibration Questions: "How am I supposed to do that?" "What about this is important?"
Tactical Empathy: Understand their world, acknowledge it.
Black Swan: Unknown unknowns that change everything.

Key insight: Never make a first offer. Use labels and mirrors to get the
other side to reveal their true position.

### 14. Straight Line System (Jordan Belfort)
3 Certainties needed to close:
1. Certainty about YOU (the salesperson)
2. Certainty about the PRODUCT (what it does)
3. Certainty about the COMPANY (stability, track record)

Loop System: When certainty drops below 50%, loop back.
Gather info, build value, raise certainty. Target: all 3 above 80%.

### 15. Cialdini 6 Principles of Influence (1984)
Reciprocity: Give first. Free audit, free report, free insight.
Commitment: Get small yeses. "Can I show you?" beats "Want to buy?"
Social Proof: Case studies, testimonials, logos.
Authority: Certifications, published work, speaking.
Liking: Find common ground, be genuine.
Scarcity: "This pricing is available until Friday" (only if true).

### 16. Voss Tactical Empathy Application
In negotiations:
- Mirror their concerns (repeat last 3 words)
- Label their fears ("It sounds like you are worried about...")
- Use calibration questions ("How should we structure this?")
- Find the Black Swan (hidden motivation)
- Never make the first concession

## PART 5: PROSPECTING FRAMEWORKS

### 17. Predictable Revenue (Aaron Ross)
Core principle: Build a predictable, repeatable sales machine.

Framework:
- Cold Calling 2.0: Warm, personalized outreach at scale
- Assembly Line Sales: Specialize roles (SDR, AE, CS)
- Lead Generation Machine: Consistent pipeline from cold outreach
- Revenue Waterfall: Leads > MQLs > SQLs > Opportunities > Closed

Key insight: "Revenue is predictable when you have a SYSTEM, not when you
have a hero salesperson."

### 18. Fanatical Prospecting (Jeb Blount)
Core principle: Fill your pipeline with consistent daily activity.

Daily Activity Targets:
- 100 dials/day (or equivalent touches)
- 15-20 conversations/day
- 3-5 qualified opportunities/week
- 2-3 closes/month

Key insight: "The pipeline is everything. If you are not prospecting every
day, you are dying."

### 19. SNAP Selling (Jill Konrath)
Keep it Simple: Your message must be dead simple (busy buyers tune out)
Be iNcredible: Be remarkable, not average
Always Align: Align with their priorities, not yours
Raise Priority: Create urgency around THEIR priorities

When to use: Selling to very busy, distracted buyers.

## PART 6: STORYTELLING FRAMEWORKS

### 20. To Sell Is Human (Daniel Pink)
Core principle: Everyone is in sales now. The rules have changed.

3 Principles:
1. Attunement: Understand the other person's perspective
2. buoyancy: Stay afloat in an ocean of rejection
3. Clarity: Make sense of a complex situation

The Pitch Types:
- One-Word Pitch: Condense your idea to one word
- Question Pitch: Ask a question that implies your solution
- Rhyming Pitch: Make it memorable (rhymes stick)
- Subject-Line Pitch: Email subject that gets opened
- Pixar Pitch: "Once upon a time... Every day... One day... Because of that... Until finally..."

### 21. Let's Get Real or Let's Not Play (Philipp Kurland)
Core principle: Radical honesty builds trust faster than manipulation.

Framework:
1. Be transparent about your product (strengths AND weaknesses)
2. Ask the customer to be equally transparent
3. If the fit is not there, walk away (builds massive trust)
4. If the fit IS there, close with confidence

Key insight: Walking away from a deal that is not right is the most powerful
closing technique in existence.

## PART 7: VALUE AND ROI FRAMEWORKS

### 22. Value-Based Selling
Value = (Benefit - Cost) / Risk

ROI Calculator:
Current cost of problem: $____/month
Time lost: ____ hours x $____/hour = $____
Revenue lost: ____ deals x $____ = $____
Total annual cost: $____
Your solution cost: $____/year
Net annual benefit: $____
ROI: ____x return

### 23. Command of the Message (Force Management)
Core principle: Know your value proposition so deeply you can articulate
it in any situation, to any stakeholder, in 30 seconds.

Framework:
1. What problems do you solve? (in their language)
2. What are the business impacts? (quantified)
3. What is your differentiator? (why you, not them)
4. What proof do you have? (case studies, numbers)
5. What is the cost of inaction? (risk of doing nothing)

## PART 8: THE COMPLETE DEAL LOOP

Research > Qualify > Discover > Present > Negotiate > Close > Fulfill > Skillify

Step 1 -- Research: Company, person, pain hypothesis, value hypothesis.
Step 2 -- Qualify: MEDDPICC >= 28/40 (or GPCTBA for inbound).
Step 3 -- Discover: SPIN or GAP Selling questions. Listen for Black Swans.
Step 4 -- Present: Challenger (Teach, Tailor, Take Control) or Pitch Anything (STRONG).
Step 5 -- Negotiate: Voss tactical empathy. Mirror, label, calibrate.
Step 6 -- Close: Belfort certainties above 80%. Sandler next step.
Step 7 -- Fulfill: Deliver what was promised. Get referral.
Step 8 -- Skillify: Document what worked. Build playbook.

## PART 9: OBJECTION HANDLING

1. "Too expensive" -- GAP: "What is the cost of NOT doing this?"
2. "Need to think" -- Calibration: "What specifically do you need to think about?"
3. "Not the right time" -- Implication: "What happens if you wait 6 months?"
4. "Already have a solution" -- Challenger: "Here is something yours does not address..."
5. "Need to talk to boss" -- Coach: "What does your boss care about most?"
6. "No budget" -- Economic Buyer: "Who controls the budget for solving X?"
7. "Just not interested" -- Kurland: "I understand. Most of our best clients said the same."

---

## PART 10: PIPELINE MANAGEMENT

Stage Definitions:
1. Research (100% -> 2): Company fits ICP, pain hypothesis
2. Qualify (60% -> 3): MEDDPICC >= 28/40
3. Discover (50% -> 4): Pain quantified, Champion identified
4. Present (50% -> 5): Solution mapped, ROI shown
5. Negotiate (60% -> 6): Terms discussed, timeline set
6. Close (80% -> 7): Signed, payment received
7. Fulfilled (100%): Delivered, referral obtained

---

## ANTI-PATTERNS
- Using the wrong framework for the situation (use Situation Selector)
- Qualifying with BANT alone (use MEDDPICC or GPCTBA)
- Talking more than listening (rep who talks least wins)
- Presenting before establishing pain (SPIN violation)
- No Champion (cannot win without one inside)
- Not quantifying value (ROI is not optional)
- Supplicating instead of framing (Pitch Anything violation)
- Not checkpointing before high-stakes calls
